02 A school day

Routine, with room to breathe.

The day is deliberately predictable. Children know what comes next, which is why the noise in the courtyard at half past seven is excitement rather than chaos.

  • 07:30

    Gates open

    Supervised play in the courtyard; nursery children go straight to their classrooms.

  • 07:55

    Assembly

    Two minutes of notices, one song, a short thought on the value of the month.

  • 08:05

    Lessons begin

    Four periods, a twenty-minute break with fruit, then three more periods.

  • 12:30

    Lunch and clubs

    Hot lunch for those enrolled, then club time: choir, football, reading, chess, dance.

  • 15:30

    Day ends

    Classes are dismissed by the class teacher to a named guardian or bus monitor.

The courtyard, twenty minutes before assembly.

03 Clubs & societies

Something to belong to.

Clubs meet three afternoons a week and are free. Children choose at the start of each term; places are capped so a club is a group, not an audience.

Reading club

Thirty minutes of quiet reading, then a discussion. Runs in both languages on alternating weeks.

Debate & public speaking

Classes 4 to 6 argue motions in English and French, with a termly debate against a visiting school.

Science club

Experiments that do not fit the timetable, and the projects entered for the February science fair.

Choir

Forty voices, two languages, one rehearsal a week. Sings at assembly, the Christmas concert and graduation.

Chess club

Twelve boards, a ladder and a school championship in March. Open to Classes 3 and above.

Environment club

Tree planting, the school garden and the waste separation scheme that the club runs itself.

04 Sports programmes

Four houses, one field.

Every pupil belongs to one of four houses — Bamoun, Njitapon, Mfomchuet and Nguon — and houses compete all year for the sports cup. Physical education is timetabled twice a week for every class, including nursery, where it looks mostly like running and laughing.

05 Arts & culture

Foumban is in the curriculum.

The town's craft and court traditions are not decoration here. Children learn to draw the patterns, hear the stories and sing the songs of the kingdom they live in.

Visual arts

Drawing, painting and the geometric patterns of Bamoun beadwork, taught by a local craftsperson each term.

Music & dance

Choir, drumming and traditional dance, with a performance at the Nguon celebrations when the school is invited.

Drama

A bilingual nativity play each December and a Class 6 production at the end of the year.

Heritage days

Once a term, pupils present a piece of Bamoun history, a proverb or a family story to their class.

06 Leadership programmes

Responsibility, given early and taken seriously.

Every Class 6 pupil holds a responsibility for the year: house captain, library monitor, assembly leader, sports organiser, or buddy to a Nursery 1 child. The roles are real — the library monitor decides the display, the assembly leader writes the notices.

  • Elected house captains and vice-captains in September.
  • Reading buddies: every Class 5 pupil is paired with a Nursery 2 child.
  • Junior council meets monthly with the head teacher and brings class concerns.
  • Peer mediators, trained in Class 5, help settle playground disputes.
Why it matters. Children who have held a small office at eleven are far less frightened by the first week of secondary school at twelve.

07 Community service

Something given back each term.

Service is timetabled, not suggested. Each class plans one project a term with its teacher and reports on it in assembly.

Term 1

The Njitapon clean-up

Classes 4 to 6 spend a Friday morning clearing the market approach with the quarter head and the waste service.

Term 2

Reading at the palace library

Pupils read aloud to younger children from the quarter on Saturday mornings, in English and French.

Term 3

Visits to elders

Small groups visit older residents with fruit and a song, and write down one story they are told.
