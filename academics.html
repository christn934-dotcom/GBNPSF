02 Curriculum overview

What is taught, and in which language.

Language of instruction shifts by subject and by year. Children are never asked to switch language in the middle of a difficult idea.

Nursery 1 & 2 · ages 3–5

Language of instruction: both, with a lead teacher in each language and a bilingual classroom assistant.

The nursery day is built around play, story and routine. Children learn to recognise and write their names, count and compare to twenty, follow two-step instructions in either language, and sit still for a story. Music, movement and outdoor play are timetabled daily, not used as rewards.

Assessment: observation-based, recorded against six developmental strands and shared with guardians each term.

Primary · Classes 1–6 · ages 6–11

Language of instruction: English for mathematics, science and social studies; French for French language, literature and civics. English language and French language are taught as separate subjects every day.

From Class 1, pupils follow the national primary curriculum with additional reading time. By Class 3 they write extended pieces in both languages. Classes 5 and 6 prepare for the common entrance examination with weekly practice papers from January.

Assessment: continuous assessment (40%) and end-of-term examination (60%), reported on a single bilingual report card.

Secondary preparation · Forms 1–4 · ages 12–15

Language of instruction: English and French streams, with pupils able to take the other language as a subject.

Offered progressively as cohorts graduate. Subject teaching replaces the class-teacher model, with laboratory work in science and a written research task each year. The aim is that pupils enter a lycée or college already used to note-taking, independent reading and deadlines.

Assessment: subject examinations each term plus a project mark; mock examinations in Form 4.

Advanced studies · ages 16–18

Language of instruction: English or French, following the pupil's examination route.

A small revision programme for graduates of the school preparing for the GCE A-level or the baccalauréat: supervised study, weekly mock papers, and guidance on university and scholarship applications.

Assessment: weekly timed papers, marked against examination board criteria and discussed one-to-one.

A note on language. A child who arrives speaking only French is not placed behind in English, and the reverse is also true. Support classes run twice a week in the first year for pupils joining the second language late. Ask the office for the current language-support timetable.

03 Departments

Six departments, one timetable.

Heads of department meet weekly to align what is being taught in each language, so a topic is not repeated in October and abandoned in November.

Languages & literacy

English, French, reading, handwriting and library lessons. Nine teachers, including two reading specialists.

Mathematics

Number, measurement, geometry and problem solving, with weekly mental arithmetic for every class.

Science

Integrated science in primary; biology, chemistry and physics from Form 1, taught in the science room.

Social studies & civics

Cameroonian and Bamoun history, geography, citizenship and the constitution, in both languages.

Arts, music & culture

Drawing, craft, choir, traditional dance and drama. The choir sings at assembly and at the Christmas concert.

Physical education

Two sessions a week plus inter-house competition in football, handball, athletics and traditional games.

06 Examinations

How we mark, and what it counts for.

Assessment is continuous, not only terminal. A pupil's term grade combines work done across the term with the end-of-term examination, which is why a single bad afternoon in December cannot erase four months of effort.

Term weighting
ComponentWeightHow it is set
Continuous assessment40%Two class tests, homework and a reading record per term
End-of-term examination60%Written papers set by heads of department and moderated
Character reportSeparateCourtesy, effort, punctuality and service, described in words

Examination rules

  • Pupils arrive by 07:40 on examination days; papers begin at 08:00.
  • No téléphone, watch or written note may enter the examination room.
  • A pupil absent through illness may sit the paper on the published catch-up date with a medical note.
  • Marks are published on the portal within ten working days; printed reports follow at the end of term.
  • Queries about a mark are raised with the class teacher within five days of the report.

National examinations

Class 6 pupils sit the common entrance examination for secondary schools. Registration is handled by the school office in February, and the fee is billed separately from tuition. Mock examinations run in January, March and April.

Results online. When the results module goes live, guardians will check marks with the student ID printed on the report slip. Until then, results are collected from the office.
